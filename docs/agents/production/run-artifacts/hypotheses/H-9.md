# H-9 — verify-width cost curve ms(k), k = 1..8

Probe: DISCOVERY · host `rasputin` · card PCI `07:00.0` (`HIP_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1`, 42 CU reported by HIP,
gfx1100, 19.98 GiB) · 2026-10-23 · scratch `~/a9/` · harness copy `~/a9/iq3s_valid_k.cpp`
(from the read-only `~/e1/iq3s_valid.cpp`).

## Question and the decision it feeds

One decode step reads the weights once. Speculative verification proposes `k` tokens and scores all `k`
against that single read, so `ms(k)` — not `ms(1)` — is the divisor in the value of drafting:
`value(k) = acceptance(k) / ms(k)`.

**Answer, measured:** `ms(k) = 1.764 + 0.588·k` ms at 1.0700 GiB. The weight read is paid once
(≈ 1.76 ms of the 2.35 ms step); **each extra verification token costs 0.51–0.72 ms, median 0.57 ms, and
that marginal cost does not grow with k.** `ms(8)/ms(1) = 2.75`, so 8 tokens cost 2.75× one token, not 8×:
per-token time falls 2.35 → 0.81 ms (2.9×). Emitted instructions per token fall 193 → 52.6 words per 32
weights per lane. Depth is therefore cheap per token but not free, and the arithmetic below puts the
optimum at **k≈4–5** for acceptances of 3/4–4/5, i.e. shallower than the common default of 5–8.

**The one kernel change.** `k_fullk<LAY,K>` is `k_full` with `K` integer accumulators and `K` activation
vectors: the block read, the five quant-field offsets, the addressing (`pb = row + (b>>3)*110`), the
codebook gather, the sign application, the scale application and the checksum term are unchanged; only the
`dp4a` chain is replicated `K` times per 32-weight group. The activation vectors are `K` copies of the
harness's int8 vector (vector 0 byte-identical to the original, vectors 1..7 offset by `+53j` in the
`(i*37+11)%199-99` generator). Because vector 0 is unchanged, the k=1 checksum must equal the legacy
`full` checksum exactly — it does, in every run (`gate: vector0 total == legacy dot total ? yes`).

## Correctness

Tolerance **stated before the run**: for every (k, activation vector, row) checked,
`|y_gpu − y_fp64| / (|y_fp64| + 1e-30) ≤ 2e-3`. This is the threshold the harness has always used for
`full` vs fp64 (which measures 3.2e-06, three orders of margin).

Two independent gates per k, per run:

1. **fp64 reference (`KVERIFY`)** — 8 rows × k vectors, the CPU reference recomputed in the same binary
   from the same GGUF bytes, stride-correct per vector.

Least-squares over the eight points: **ms(k) = 1.7639 + 0.5877·k** (k=4 predicted 4.115 vs 4.203 measured,
−2.1 %). The fitted marginal (0.588 ms) and the measured median marginal (0.567 ms) agree.
`ms(8) = 6.465 ms` vs `8·ms(1) = 18.81 ms` — **ratio 2.749, sub-linear by the brief's ≤ 8 test by a wide
margin.** k=8 is the noisiest point (one of three runs gave 6.06 ms with a 9.1 % intra-run spread); the
other two give 6.47 and 6.62, and the median is stable. Total run-to-run spread is ≤ 1.8 % for k=1..7.

**On the GB/s column, because the brief's expectation and the physics disagree and the data must win.**
The brief predicts the GB/s equivalent *rises* with k. Under the definition the brief itself gives
(bytes read × passes ÷ time) it **falls**, 489 → 178 GB/s, and it has to: the same 1.07 GiB is read once
either way, and the step gets longer as more dot work is stacked on it. It is the *work-equivalent* column
(bytes × k ÷ time) that rises, 489 → 1422 GB/s. That column is a ratio, not a DRAM measurement, and it
cannot be compared to the 776 GB/s wall or the 645 GB/s memory-free ceiling — at k=8 each byte is used
8 times, so 1422 GB/s of "work" is 178 GB/s of DRAM plus 8× reuse. The wall check applies to the raw
column and the raw column passes it at every k (max 489, 63 % of the wall). Nothing here reads faster
than the card can deliver; what changes with k is that the kernel walks away from memory-bound
(63 % of the wall at k=1) toward issue-bound (23 % of the wall at k=8).

Reading the growth: the second vector adds **26** words, every further vector **31–40** (median 32):
`+8 v_dot4_i32_i8` and `+8 global_load_b32` (the second x-vector) plus the `s_waitcnt`/`s_delay_alu`
that the scheduler inserts for the new dependency chains (`s_waitcnt` 19 → 75, i.e. +8 per vector).
The **non-replicated** part of the 193-word body is the dequant: 48 sign + 34 index + 9 address + 11
codebook gather ≈ 102 words, which `k` vectors share. So verification converts the kernel from
"≈100 words of unpack per ≈16 words of dot" (k=1) to "≈100 words of unpack per ≈128 words of dot" (k=8):
words per 32 weights 193 → 421 (**2.18× for 8× the tokens**), words per token 193 → 52.6 (**3.7× cheaper**).

Instruction growth is *linear* in k (+32/vector) while time per token falls *sub*-linearly in instruction
terms (2.9× per token vs 3.7× fewer words per token) — the extra instructions are not free, but a
`v_dot4_i32_i8` is 1/24 of the k=1 body, so there is a large constant to amortise and that is exactly
what the marginals show.

## Reading it

- **The curve is affine, not asymptotic.** Above k=1 the marginal is flat at 0.51–0.72 ms (median 0.567)
  with no upward trend: `ms(k) − ms(k−1)` at k=8 (0.515) is *smaller* than at k=2 (0.721). There is no
  "knee": the weight read (1.76 ms) is paid once and then each token costs the same increment. The
  per-token *average* is what keeps improving, and it improves as `1.76/k + 0.59`.
- **Where the return stops being worth it is a value question, not a cost question.** The k-th token's
  cost is constant; its expected accepted contribution `α^k` decays geometrically. That is the whole
  optimum, and it is why the answer is k≈4–5 and not k=8.
- **Per-step cost at the target model.** This measures the IQ3_S class (3.39 GiB of the 10.57 GiB file,
  32 %). Holding the other 7.56 GiB at the harness's 752 GB/s k=1 cost (the conservative assumption:
  a memory-bound class does not amortise its read into more tokens), a whole-file step is 7.42 + 10.79 =
  **18.2 ms at k=1 (54.9 tok/s)**. Scaling only the IQ3_S part by the measured curve: k=4 → 24.1 ms/step,
  k=6 → 27.6 ms, k=8 → 31.2 ms. At acceptance 3/4 that is 85 → 89 → 87 accepted tok/s for k = 4 / 6 / 8;
  i.e. **the whole-file gain saturates around k=5–6 even before acceptance is applied**, because
  68 % of the read does not benefit. That projection is a bound, not a measurement: the 7.56 GiB is a
  different quant class whose own ms(k) is not measured here (see limits).

## Verdict — the depth the arithmetic favours

Per-step value `V(k) = A(k)/ms(k)` with `A(k) = Σ_{i=1..k} α^i` (per-token acceptance `α`), using the
measured `ms(k)`. Equivalent first-order rule: **go one deeper while `α^{k+1} > 0.567 ms × V(k)`.**

| acceptance | A(4) | A(5) | A(6) | best k | value at best | V(k=1) | V(k=8) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3/4 (α=0.750) | 2.051 | 2.288 | 2.466 | **4** | 488 tok/s | 319 | 418 |
| 4/5 (α=0.800) | 2.362 | 2.689 | 2.951 | **5** | 565 tok/s | 340 | 515 |
| 5/6 (α=0.833) | 2.589 | 2.991 | 3.326 | **5** (k=6 within 0.7 %) | 628 tok/s | 354 | 594 |

Worked example, α=0.75: at k=3 one more token costs 0.5361 ms × V(3)=0.4730 acc/ms → 0.2536 accepted tokens
of budget, against an expected gain of `α^4 = 0.3164` → draft deeper. At k=4 the budget is
0.5563 × 0.4880 = 0.2715 vs `α^5 = 0.2373` → **stop at 4.** Everything past that is a loss on this hardware.

**The decision this feeds, stated as numbers:** at acceptance 3/4, depth 5 costs 0.556 ms/step more than
depth 4 and buys 0.237 expected tokens; it would need the fifth-position acceptance to be above **0.77**
(0.2715^0.2) to break even, versus the 0.75 being modelled. At acceptance 4/5, depth 5 is the optimum and
depth 6 is within 1 %. **Drag depth should be set from the measured acceptance curve, and on this engine the
frontier sits at k = 4–6, with a marginal verification budget of 0.567 ms per additional drafted token** —
that is the number a drafter's own per-token cost has to beat. The drafter's cost is *not* included in any
figure above; it is added, not amortised, and with 0.567 ms of headroom per step a drafter must produce a
draft token in under ~0.57 ms (≈ 1 760 draft tokens/s) for any depth to pay at all.

## Limits

1. **Absolute scale ±3 %** (card state, §gate). Ratios and marginals are unaffected; `ms(k)` in
   milliseconds would shift by the same factor at every k.
2. **Synthetic activations.** The k vectors are the harness's int8 vector and 7 offsets of it. Nothing here
   measures acceptance — only the cost side of `acceptance(k)/ms(k)`. Real activation int8 distributions
   change nothing in the addressing or the op count, but they are not this data.
3. **k=8 is the noisiest point** (8.7 % across runs, 9.1 % within one run). The median is used; a single
   run of k=8 is worth ±0.3 ms.
4. **One quant class.** IQ3_S only (32 % of the file). The identity of `a` and `b` in `ms(k) = a + b·k`
   for the other 7.56 GiB (4–4.5 bpw types) is unmeasured; the whole-file figures above are bounds built on
   the harness's 752 GB/s k=1 figure for that class.
5. The harness's `exp_nosgn` row (lossy expansion, an H-1 diagnostic) reads 787 GB/s = 101.4 % of the
   776 wall and passes the harness's own 2 % tolerance; it stays in the logs but is not one of the rows
   this report draws on.
